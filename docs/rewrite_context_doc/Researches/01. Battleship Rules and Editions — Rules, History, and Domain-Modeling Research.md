# Battleship Rules and Editions — Rules, History, and Domain-Modeling Research

## Executive summary

The most important conclusion is that **there is no single universal “Battleship ruleset.”** The Milton Bradley/Hasbro lineage, the 1931 *Salvo* game, customary Eastern-European *Sea Battle / Морський бій / Морской бой*, and later electronic/video editions share a hidden-fleet coordinate-guessing core, but they differ on fleet composition, whether ships may touch, whether diagonal placement is allowed, whether a hit grants another shot, the amount of information disclosed after a hit, salvo mechanics, board geometry, and even victory procedure. The historical record supports treating these as a **family of related rulesets**, not as one ruleset with cosmetic edition names. citeturn30view0turn31view4turn31view2turn32view0

For the classic Milton Bradley/Hasbro plastic game, the strongest primary evidence is unambiguous on the turn question: **a normal turn consists of one shot, and the turn ends after that shot whether it is a hit or a miss. A player does not get another shot merely because they hit or sink a ship.** This is explicit in the 1990 Milton Bradley rules and remains explicit in later Hasbro rules. citeturn2view1turn31view2

The familiar five-ship fleet also changed names across editions. The 1990 Milton Bradley rules use **Carrier 5, Battleship 4, Cruiser 3, Submarine 3, Destroyer 2**. The 2002 Hasbro rules use **Carrier 5, Battleship 4, Destroyer 3, Submarine 3, Patrol Boat 2**. A 2016/2017 Hasbro B1817 edition contains only four ships per player: **Carrier 5, Battleship 4, Submarine 3, Patrol Boat 2**. Current F4527 material has returned to the five-ship Carrier/Battleship/Destroyer/Submarine/Patrol Boat lineup. citeturn2view0turn2view3turn31view2turn23view0turn37view0

The project's name **`MILTON_BRADLEY` is too broad** to identify one exact ruleset. Milton Bradley sold the plastic game from 1967, and even within the documented lineage there are differences such as fleet names and starting-player procedure. An exact identifier such as `MILTON_BRADLEY_1990` is much safer. The Strong National Museum of Play dates the plastic Milton Bradley *Battleship* to 1967, while Hasbro's later Retro Series recreates a “1967 Edition”; that modern recreation should not be treated as a verbatim archival copy of every original 1967 rule without further evidence. citeturn30view0turn3view1

The project's **`UKRAINIAN` name is even less defensible as an edition name**. I found no Hasbro/Milton Bradley or Ukrainian industry authority defining an official national “Ukrainian ruleset.” In fact, two Ukrainian manufacturers illustrate the problem: Arial sells a game titled *Морський бій* whose description says a player who hits continues shooting, whereas Artos Games sells a *Морський бій* with **20 ships, dice, movement, firing ranges and mines**—a fundamentally different game. Thus “Ukrainian” denotes neither a standardized national edition nor a stable rule specification. citeturn26view0turn26view1turn28view1turn28view2turn28view3

If the current project uses `UKRAINIAN` for the familiar paper-game rules—10×10 grid, fleet of one length-4, two length-3, three length-2 and four length-1 ships, no touching even diagonally, and another shot after a hit—then the defensible canonical name is something like **`SEA_BATTLE_10_SHIP`** or, if the behavior must be completely explicit, **`SEA_BATTLE_10_SHIP_NO_TOUCH_HIT_CONTINUES`**. Contemporary Ukrainian-language implementations document exactly this family of rules, but the evidence supports calling it customary *Sea Battle*, not an official Ukrainian edition. citeturn22search1turn25search6

For the browser application, the rules engine should therefore be **data/policy driven**. Board dimensions, fleet multiset, ship geometry, adjacency policy, firing mode, hit-turn policy, result-disclosure policy, duplicate-shot policy and victory policy belong in an immutable `Ruleset`, rather than being scattered through `Game`, `Ship`, or controller code. This is especially important because even official Hasbro products range from ordinary single-shot play to Salvo, electronic special weapons, configurable layouts, card powers, simultaneous ball-tossing, and licensed themed versions. citeturn31view2turn32view0turn32view1turn32view4turn32view5

## History, official names, and regional terminology

**Verified history.** The Strong National Museum of Play traces relevant antecedents to E. I. Horsman's 1890 *Basilinda*, reports a similar hand-drawn-grid game among Russian officers around World War I, identifies Starex Novelty Company's **1931 *Salvo*** as an early printed-grid commercial version, identifies Milton Bradley's **1943 *Broadsides: The Game of Naval Strategy*** as a pencil-and-paper version, and dates Milton Bradley's plastic **Battleship** to **1967**. The museum also documents a 1977 *Electronic Battleship* artifact and later computer/electronic derivatives. This is reputable historical synthesis, but *Basilinda* should be described as a precursor or parallel rather than asserted to have exactly the later Battleship rules.  
Direct source: <https://www.museumofplay.org/blog/its-a-hit-battleship-cruises-into-the-national-toy-hall-of-fame/>, especially the historical discussion corresponding to lines 195–206. citeturn30view0

There is also earlier Russian-language evidence for the **name/concept “sea battle.”** The Prozhito corpus contains a 1907 diary entry by Rurik Ivnev referring to playing “sea battle” (*морской бой*). That is significant because it predates World War I, but the entry does **not** describe the board, fleet, placement or turn rules, so it cannot establish that the modern ten-ship paper rules already existed in 1907.  
Direct source: <https://corpus.prozhito.org/note/225011>. citeturn13search1

**Salvo is an actual historical product name, not merely the name of Hasbro's later multi-shot option.** The surviving 1931 Starex rule scan is titled *SALVO — The Game of Wits* and carries a 1931 Starex Novelty Company copyright. Its rules differ materially from modern Hasbro Salvo: it has four ships, permits diagonal ships, begins with six shots rather than one per ship, and reduces later salvo strength by weighted penalties as ships are sunk.  
Direct primary scan: <https://www.gamecatalog.org/rules/StarexNoveltyCo_Salvo.pdf>, especially pp. 2–5. citeturn9view1turn10view0turn10view1turn9view4

| **Salvo** | Official 1931 Starex product title. Later also the name Hasbro uses for its advanced multi-shot Battleship variant. Those two mechanics are not identical. citeturn31view4turn31view2 | Use `STAREX_SALVO_1931` for the historical game and `HASBRO_SALVO_*` for Hasbro's variant. | **Sea Battle / Морський бій / Морской бой** | Generic/regional name for a family of naval guessing games and also a title independently used by Ukrainian manufacturers whose mechanics differ. citeturn26view0turn26view1 | Treat as a family/category, not a unique ruleset. |

A useful historical distinction follows: **product name, language name, and ruleset are three different concepts**. “Battleship” can refer to the brand while its fleet changes; “Sea Battle” can cover several incompatible games; and “Salvo” can mean either the 1931 Starex game or a later Hasbro firing mode. That distinction should be reflected directly in the domain model. citeturn31view4turn31view2turn26view1

Two especially important conclusions follow from this comparison.

First, **“hit grants another turn” is not a Battleship universal**. It is false for the documented Milton Bradley/Hasbro normal game, where a hit ends the turn, while it is true of documented Ukrainian *Морський бій* implementations such as Arial and the contemporary customary-style app. citeturn2view1turn31view2turn26view0turn22search1

Second, **“ships may not touch” is also not universal**. The reviewed classic Hasbro rules forbid overlap and diagonal ship orientation but do not impose the one-cell halo characteristic of the common ten-ship Sea Battle rules. The 1931 Starex rules go further and explicitly allow strategic grouping and diagonal vessel orientation. citeturn2view0turn10view0turn22search1

## Analysis of `UKRAINIAN`, `MILTON_BRADLEY`, and canonical terminology

**Verified fact — `MILTON_BRADLEY` is historically meaningful but mechanically underspecified.** Milton Bradley is the publisher associated with the plastic 1967 game, and the 1990 rulebook is a Milton Bradley publication. But an enum called `MILTON_BRADLEY` does not say whether it means the original 1967 product, a later product 4730 rulebook, or some other MB-era release. Even the documented starting-player rule differs between Hasbro's Retro 1967 recreation and the 1990 rulebook. citeturn30view0turn2view1turn5view0

**Recommendation:** rename it according to the exact behavior you intend to reproduce:

`MILTON_BRADLEY_1990`

would be an excellent choice if the intended fleet is Carrier 5 / Battleship 4 / Cruiser 3 / Submarine 3 / Destroyer 2 and every shot ends the turn. If compatibility with Hasbro's later naming is desired instead, use:

`HASBRO_CLASSIC_2002`

or a behavior-oriented identifier such as:

`CLASSIC_5_SHIP_ALTERNATING`

The first style is better when the goal is historical fidelity; the second is better when rules are product-owned and versioned separately from UI branding.

**Verified fact — no official ruleset corresponding to a national edition called `UKRAINIAN` was identified.** The strongest available Ukrainian evidence points in the opposite direction. Arial's Ukrainian *Морський бій* has the familiar “hit and continue” behavior, while Artos's identically titled *Морський бій* is a dice-driven, moving-fleet, mined battlefield with 20 ships. Both are legitimate Ukrainian commercial products, but they plainly do not implement one common official “Ukrainian edition.” citeturn26view0turn26view1turn28view1turn28view3

**Interpretation:** the project's `UKRAINIAN` is therefore almost certainly a **project-local label for a customary Sea Battle rules family**, not a historically or commercially official edition name. The name should not survive into the canonical domain unless the application's business requirement is specifically “our house rules named Ukrainian.”

If that ruleset is the familiar ten-ship configuration, the recommended identifier is:

`SEA_BATTLE_10_SHIP`

with its actual mechanics stored in configuration:

| Rule dimension | Recommended Sea Battle configuration |
|---|---|
| Grid | 10×10 |
| Fleet | length 4 × 1; length 3 × 2; length 2 × 3; length 1 × 4 |
| Orientation | horizontal / vertical |
| Overlap | forbidden |
| Orthogonal touching | forbidden |
| Diagonal touching | forbidden |
| Normal fire | one coordinate at a time |
| Turn after miss | pass |
| Turn after hit | shooter retains turn |
| Turn after sink | shooter retains turn |
| Win | all opponent ships sunk |

This closely describes the documented customary-style implementation, but the label should remain **Sea Battle**, not “official Ukrainian,” because the evidence establishes usage rather than a national governing rulebook. citeturn22search1turn25search6

For canonical game terminology, I recommend:

| Concept | Canonical term | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Collection of ships | `Fleet` | Consistent across the rulebooks. |
| Individual piece | `Ship` | More generic than hard-wiring vessel names. |
| Historical/type name | `ShipClass` or `ShipType` | Carrier, Battleship, Cruiser, etc. should be data, not subclasses. |
| Occupied size | `length` or `cellCount` | Names change while lengths can stay equal. |
| Successful attack on one cell | `HIT` | Publisher terminology. citeturn31view2 |
| Failed attack | `MISS` | Publisher terminology. citeturn31view2 |
| All cells of one ship hit | `SUNK` | The formal classic rulebooks say a ship is “sunk”; this is preferable to a generic `DESTROYED`. citeturn31view2 |
| Entire opponent fleet eliminated | `GameWon` / `WIN` | Separate from `ShipSunk`. |
| Own hidden grid | `OwnBoard` internally; optionally “Ocean Grid” in Hasbro-themed UI | “Ocean grid” is Hasbro rulebook terminology. citeturn31view2 |
| Knowledge of opponent | `TrackingBoard` or `TargetKnowledge` | Avoid storing a fake second full board containing secret data. |
| Specific mechanics bundle | `Ruleset` | More accurate than `Edition` alone. |
| Brand/localized presentation | `Theme` or `DisplayEdition` | Prevents rules and branding from becoming coupled. |

A particularly useful split is:

`GameDefinition = Ruleset + PresentationMetadata`

so that *Touché Coulé*, *Flottenmanöver*, or a Ukrainian UI translation need not automatically create new mechanics. Hasbro itself uses different localized titles for the brand, demonstrating why language and rules should not be the same enum. citeturn11search6turn11search2

## Salvo, electronic, video-game, alternate-grid, and special variants

The historical **1931 Starex Salvo** deserves its own ruleset rather than being implemented as a checkbox on modern Battleship. Its primary rulebook has four ships, permits diagonal orientation, starts each player at six attacks per salvo and reduces that firepower according to which vessels have been sunk. It also withholds results until the salvo has been completed and frames victory in terms of sinking the opposing fleet with the fewest salvos. citeturn10view0turn10view1turn9view4

Modern **Hasbro Salvo** is different. In the B1817 four-ship edition, the firing player starts with four shots—one per ship afloat—and loses one shot from subsequent salvos whenever one of their ships is sunk. The shots are fired one at a time and hit/miss is announced after each; an optional challenge rule suppresses disclosure of which ship was hit. citeturn31view2 The standard five-ship F4527 guide follows the same modern pattern beginning at five shots. citeturn37view0turn37view2

This means at least these properties must be edition-specific:

`salvoSizePolicy`, `salvoResolutionTiming`, `shipHitDisclosure`, `salvoDamagePenalty`, and potentially `victoryEvaluationTiming`.

**Electronic Battleship** demonstrates a still larger departure. Hasbro's A3846 product explicitly advertises **missile salvos, air raids and battle scans**, along with automatic ship layouts, electronic lights and sounds and five combat craft per side.  
Direct source: <https://instructions.hasbro.com/en-us/instruction/electronic-battleship>, Product Info. citeturn32view0

A newer Hasbro electronic product, F7565, supports **up to 64 preset layouts plus custom layouts**, solo or two-player play, and “special attacks”; the package includes 10 ships and 12 special-attack pegs.  
Direct source: <https://instructions.hasbro.com/en-us/instruction/electronic-battleship-board-game-for-families-and-kids-strategy-naval-combat-game-family-gifts-family-games-games-for-kids>, Product Info. citeturn32view1

| **U-Build Battleship** | Players build/customize fleets and stack weapons to increase attack power. citeturn33view0 | Ship shape/strength cannot always be inferred from a fixed class-length table. |
| **Battleship Card Game** | Coordinate cards plus Peg and Power cards; ship cards have special powers. citeturn32view4 | A card-game implementation is a different aggregate/rules engine, despite sharing the brand and “sink five ships” goal. |
| **Battleship Shots** | Physical ball-tossing; players may act simultaneously; three two-hole ships plus a life raft; landing the red ball in the life raft can win immediately. citeturn32view5 | “Turn-based coordinate shot” and “all ships sunk” are not brand-wide invariants. |
| **Star Wars Battleship** | Four starships per side/eight total components and Star Wars theming. citeturn33view1 | Fleet cardinality is edition-specific. |
| **Battleship Command: Pirates of the Caribbean** | Hasbro describes a **multi-level grid**, more ships and special powers. citeturn34search6 | Board topology may require more than a rectangular two-dimensional coordinate. |
| **Electronic handheld** | Automatic/manual fleet programming, three skill levels and automatic scoring; one-player product. citeturn34search2 | “Exactly two human players” is a classic-game invariant, not a whole-brand invariant. |

Hasbro has also produced more radical board redesigns. A 2008-era *Battleship: The Tactical Combat Game* is documented by board-game references as using a changed tactical board/hex-style system, but I did not recover a first-party Hasbro rule PDF for that edition during this research. It should therefore be treated as **medium-confidence secondary evidence**, rather than used to specify exact engine mechanics. This is a good example of where the research supports “alternate topology exists” more strongly than it supports every detailed rule of that particular edition. citeturn16search20

For video games, the licensed 2016 Ubisoft PlayStation release provides both a **Classic mode** and a separate **“Clash at Sea!”** mode. Its local PlayLink mode uses players' phones/tablets as private screens for secretly deploying fleets, choosing targets and firing—an especially direct precedent for the hidden-information architecture of a browser implementation. Sony's store identifies the game as licensed by Hasbro to Ubisoft.  
Direct source: <https://store.playstation.com/en-hr/product/EP0001-CUSA04286_00-HGCBATTLESHIPPS4>, description and licensing section. citeturn32view7

The software conclusion is therefore strong: the following properties **must not be hard-coded as “Battleship rules”**:

| Edition-specific policy | Why |
|---|---|
| Board width/height/topology | Classic is 10×10, but branded variants can use alternative/multi-level boards. citeturn34search6 |
| Fleet composition | 1990 = five ships; B1817 = four; Sea Battle customary = ten; themed variants differ. citeturn2view0turn31view2turn33view1 |
| Ship names | Cruiser/Destroyer/Patrol Boat naming changed between 1990 and 2002. citeturn2view0turn2view3 |
| Ship geometry | Classic Hasbro forbids diagonal; 1931 Salvo permits it; other variants can use unusual shapes. citeturn31view2turn10view0 |
| Adjacency | Common Sea Battle requires spacing; Hasbro classic does not state such a restriction. citeturn22search1turn2view0 |
| Shots per turn | One in classic, multiple in Salvo, special patterns in electronic variants. citeturn31view2turn32view0 |
| Turn after hit | End turn in Hasbro classic; retain turn in customary Sea Battle/Arial. citeturn31view2turn26view0 |
| First-player selection | “Decide” in later rules; red unit first in Retro 1967 rules. citeturn2view1turn5view0 |
| Hit disclosure | Standard classic reveals ship identity; advanced Salvo may not. citeturn31view2 |
| Sunk disclosure | Standard classic announces it; variants could conceal information. |
| Duplicate-shot handling | Paper rules seek to prevent repeats but do not specify computer-style error semantics. citeturn31view2 |
| Movement | Static in classic; explicitly present in the Artos Ukrainian game and other special variants. citeturn28view2turn28view3 |
| Victory | Entire fleet sunk in classic, but special editions may add alternate instant victories/objectives. citeturn32view5 |

## Formal domain model and state machine

For the target browser application, I recommend making **`Game` the aggregate root**, with the chosen `Ruleset` copied or referenced immutably when the game is created. A later deployment changing the ruleset catalog must not silently change the rules of an already-created anonymous game.

| Domain concept | Recommended representation | Important responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| `Game` | Entity / aggregate root | Lifecycle, seats, selected ruleset, phase, active player, winner, command validation. |
| `PlayerSeat` | Entity | One of the two anonymous positions. Do not confuse this with a registered user: there is no account requirement. |
| `Fleet` | Entity/value collection | Enforces the ruleset's required ship multiset and readiness. |
| `Ship` | Entity | Stable `shipId`, `shipClass`, occupied cells, hit cells, sunk state. Two ships of identical length must still be distinct entities. |
| `Ruleset` | Immutable value/configuration object | All mechanical variation. |
| `Coordinate` | Value object | Validated board location; should not contain assumptions such as “always A–J.” |
| `GridSpec` | Value object | Dimensions/topology plus display coordinate scheme. |
| `ShipSpec` | Value object | Type ID, display name, count, cell count/shape. |
| `Placement` | Value object | Cells occupied by a ship; preferably cells rather than only `(start, orientation, length)` so non-linear future shapes remain possible. |
| `ShotResult` | Value object | `MISS`, `HIT`, plus references appropriate to the **private** domain result. |
| `TurnPolicy` | Ruleset component | Alternation vs retain-on-hit, single vs Salvo, first-player policy. |
| `DisclosurePolicy` | Ruleset component | What the shooter learns after hit/sink/salvo. |
| `BoardProjection` | Read model | Player-specific redacted view; never expose the aggregate's complete opponent fleet. |

A robust `Ruleset` needs substantially more than an enum:

```text
Ruleset
  id
  version
  gridSpec
  fleetSpec[]
  placementPolicy
      allowedShapes/orientations
      overlapPolicy
      adjacencyPolicy
      movableAfterStart
  firingPolicy
      SINGLE | SALVO | SPECIAL
      shotsPerTurnPolicy
      duplicateShotPolicy
      salvoResolutionTiming
  turnPolicy
      initialPlayerPolicy
      afterMiss
      afterHit
      afterSink
  disclosurePolicy
      revealHitMiss
      revealShipClassOnHit
      revealShipClassOnSink
      salvoDisclosureMode
  victoryPolicy
```

The distinction between **`afterHit` and `afterSink`** is deliberate. Current Hasbro classic happens to give the same answer to both—end the turn—but keeping them separate prevents the engine from assuming that every future ruleset treats them identically. The evidence already demonstrates similar variation in firing and disclosure policies. citeturn31view2turn10view0

Recommended commands are:

`CreateGame(rulesetId)`, `JoinGame(joinToken)`, `PlaceShip`, `RemoveShip`, `LockFleet`, `FireShot`, `FireSalvo`, `ResignGame`, and the product-lifecycle command `ExpireGame`.

`UnlockFleet` is reasonable only before combat and only if the product wants an “unready” feature. Once battle begins, classic rules do not permit fleet rearrangement. citeturn2view0

Recommended domain events are:

`GameCreated`, `OpponentJoined`, `ShipPlaced`, `FleetLocked`, `BattleStarted`, `ShotDeclared`, `ShotResolved`, `ShipSunk`, `SalvoDeclared`, `SalvoResolved`, `TurnAdvanced`, `GameWon`, `PlayerResigned`, and `GameExpired`.

A final hit should normally produce a single atomic event sequence conceptually equivalent to:

`ShotResolved(HIT) → ShipSunk → GameWon`

with **no subsequent `TurnAdvanced`**.

The rendered state-machine diagram accompanying this report uses the following phase model:

**`WAITING_FOR_OPPONENT → PLACEMENT → READY_WAIT → IN_PROGRESS → FINISHED`**, with `EXPIRED` as a product lifecycle terminal state.

The important transitions are:

| Current state | Command / condition | Next state |
|---|---|---|
| `WAITING_FOR_OPPONENT` | Second anonymous seat joins | `PLACEMENT` |
| `PLACEMENT` | Place/move/remove ships | `PLACEMENT` |
| `PLACEMENT` | One valid fleet locked | `READY_WAIT` |
| `READY_WAIT` | Other player still editing | `READY_WAIT` |
| `READY_WAIT` | Both required fleets valid and locked | `IN_PROGRESS` |
| `IN_PROGRESS` | Legal non-terminal shot/salvo | `IN_PROGRESS`, with active player derived from edition's `TurnPolicy` |
| `IN_PROGRESS` | Final required enemy ship is sunk | `FINISHED` |
| `IN_PROGRESS` | Resignation/forfeit | `FINISHED` |
| nonterminal | Temporary-game TTL policy triggers | `EXPIRED` |

`EXPIRED`, inactivity timeouts, anonymous join links and resignation semantics are **application lifecycle policies**, not historical Battleship rules. Keeping them outside `Ruleset` avoids confusing product concerns with game mechanics.

### Hidden-information model

The most important information-security invariant is that the server's authoritative aggregate and the client-visible state must not be the same object. Classic rules explicitly require hidden fleet locations, and electronic/video implementations likewise preserve a private deployment/targeting view. citeturn31view2turn32view7

A useful projection model is:

**Owner projection:** own ship positions, damage to own ships, own shot history, permitted information about opponent.

**Opponent projection:** never receives unshot enemy coordinates; receives only `MISS`, `HIT`, `SUNK`, ship identity, or aggregated Salvo information to the extent the selected `DisclosurePolicy` allows.

**Public/lifecycle projection:** ruleset ID, phase, readiness, whose turn it is, and terminal result; no hidden fleet geometry.

An internal event such as:

`ShotResolved(target=D4, result=HIT, shipId=abc123, shipClass=DESTROYER)`

must **not** automatically be serialized to both clients. Under ordinary Hasbro rules, the attacker may be told “Destroyer”; under advanced Salvo, ship identity may be hidden; under a stricter variant, even more may be withheld. citeturn31view2

For an anonymous browser game, possession of a private **seat token** effectively establishes authority to see one player's hidden projection. The join/invite token and the already-claimed private seat token should therefore be separate concepts. This is an application-security recommendation rather than a historical game rule.

## Recommended application rules, test invariants, edge cases, and unresolved conflicts

For the first production version, I recommend supporting **two explicit baseline rulesets plus an optional Salvo ruleset**, rather than maintaining the current ambiguous enum names.

| Recommended ID | Intended rules |
|---|---|
| **`HASBRO_CLASSIC_2002`** | 10×10; Carrier 5, Battleship 4, Destroyer 3, Submarine 3, Patrol Boat 2; horizontal/vertical only; overlap forbidden; no additional one-cell spacing restriction; one shot per turn; hit and miss both end the turn; ship identity disclosed on hit; sunk announced; all five sunk = win. These mechanics are directly supported by Hasbro's 2002 lineage and current five-ship products. citeturn2view3turn2view4turn37view2 |
| **`SEA_BATTLE_10_SHIP`** | 10×10; lengths `4×1, 3×2, 2×3, 1×4`; H/V; no overlap; ships may not touch orthogonally or diagonally; miss passes turn; hit/sink retains turn; all ten ships sunk = win. This should be documented as a chosen customary Sea Battle profile, **not** an official national Ukrainian standard. citeturn22search1turn25search6 |
| **`HASBRO_SALVO_2002`** | Same five-ship classic fleet, but shots per turn equal own surviving ships, decreasing as ships are sunk; make disclosure mode explicit. The ruleset should be separate from normal classic play rather than an ad-hoc controller flag. citeturn31view2turn37view0 |

If exact historical Milton Bradley compatibility is required, add **`MILTON_BRADLEY_1990`** separately, with the 1990 names **Cruiser 3** and **Destroyer 2** instead of the later Destroyer 3 / Patrol Boat 2 naming. citeturn2view0

For user-visible names, I would use:

**Battleship — Hasbro Classic**  
**Sea Battle — 10-Ship Classic**  
**Battleship — Salvo**

and reserve the precise year/product identifier for API and persistence metadata. This makes the behavioral difference intelligible without falsely presenting “Ukrainian” as an official edition.

### Domain invariants suitable for automated tests

| Invariant | Suggested test assertion |
|---|---|
| **Valid coordinate** | Every occupied or fired coordinate belongs to `GridSpec`. Reject anything outside it before domain state changes. |
| **Required fleet multiset** | A player cannot lock their fleet unless each `ShipSpec.quantity` is satisfied exactly. |
| **Ship geometry** | Every ship's cells satisfy the ruleset's shape/orientation policy. Classic Hasbro ships must form a contiguous horizontal or vertical line. citeturn31view2 |
| **No overlap** | No cell is occupied by two ships where `overlapPolicy=FORBIDDEN`. |
| **Adjacency policy** | For `NO_TOUCH_8_NEIGHBOR`, every cell belonging to another ship must have Chebyshev distance greater than 1; do **not** apply this test to Hasbro classic. |
| **Fleet immutability** | Once battle starts, ordinary classic placement commands fail without modifying state. citeturn2view0 |
| **Both sides ready** | `BattleStarted` can occur only after two valid fleets are locked. |
| **Correct actor** | Only the active player can fire in turn-based rulesets. |
| **Correct target** | A player can attack only the opposing board. |
| **Single coordinate resolution** | A coordinate has exactly one authoritative first resolution; recommendation: repeated calls are rejected without consuming a turn. |
| **Result correctness** | `HIT ⇔ target coordinate is occupied by a surviving/previously hit ship cell`; otherwise `MISS`. |
| **Monotonic damage** | Once a ship cell is hit, it never becomes unhit in static classic rules. |
| **Sinking equivalence** | `ship.sunk == all(ship.cells are hit)`. |
| **Single sink event** | `ShipSunk(shipId)` is emitted exactly once. |
| **Classic turn transition** | In `HASBRO_CLASSIC_*`, every non-terminal resolved shot changes active player, regardless of HIT/MISS/SUNK. citeturn31view2 |
| **Sea Battle turn transition** | In `SEA_BATTLE_10_SHIP`, MISS changes player; HIT/SUNK retains shooter, by the application's chosen profile. citeturn22search1 |
| **Salvo capacity** | Number of declared attacks equals the ruleset's permitted salvo count for that turn. |
| **Modern Hasbro Salvo degradation** | With `n` own ships afloat, next salvo has `n` shots. citeturn31view2 |
| **Victory equivalence** | In standard elimination rules, `winner != null` iff every required opposing ship has sunk, except resignation/forfeit. |
| **Terminality** | No placement or fire command can mutate a `FINISHED` or `EXPIRED` game. |
| **Atomic final shot** | Final hit leaves ship sunk and game finished in one committed state; no intermediate externally observable “sunk but game still active” state. |
| **No turn after victory** | Final sinking attack does not emit or apply `TurnAdvanced`. |
| **Private projection** | Opponent projection never contains an unshot hidden ship coordinate. |
| **Disclosure compliance** | A ruleset with `revealShipClassOnHit=false` never leaks `shipClass` through API payloads, events, errors, WebSocket messages, logs sent to client, or UI state. |
| **Anonymous-seat isolation** | A seat credential for Player A can never retrieve Player B's private fleet projection. |
| **Ruleset immutability** | Changing the rules catalog after game creation does not change a persisted in-progress game's behavior; persist `rulesetId + version` or an immutable snapshot. |

### Edge-case catalog

| Edge case | Correct treatment |
|---|---|
| **Two Hasbro ships touch side-to-side** | Accept for the proposed Hasbro classic implementation. The reviewed rulebooks prohibit overlap but contain no Sea-Battle-style separation halo. citeturn2view0turn31view2 |
| **Two Sea Battle ships touch only at corners** | Reject when implementing the customary no-touch profile. citeturn22search1 |
| **Auto-marking cells around a sunk ship** | Safe only for a ruleset whose no-touch rule logically proves those cells empty. Never do this in Hasbro classic, because adjacent ships may occupy them. |
| **Diagonal ship** | Reject in Hasbro classic; potentially legal in 1931 Starex Salvo. citeturn31view2turn10view0 |
| **Ship along board boundary** | Legal if all occupied cells are on-grid; being at the edge is not itself prohibited. |
| **Shot already fired** | Historical rules aim to avoid repeats but do not define software error semantics. Recommendation: return `ALREADY_TARGETED`, make no state change and do not consume the turn. citeturn31view2 |
| **Hit on final intact cell of a ship** | Emit/derive `HIT` and `SUNK` from the same shot. |
| **Final intact cell of final ship** | Resolve `HIT → SUNK → GAME_WON` atomically; do not change turns afterward. |
| **Two length-3 ships** | They must be separate `Ship` entities even if their geometry is identical; type and identity are different concepts. |
| **Four one-cell Sea Battle ships** | `Ship` cannot assume length ≥2 or require an orientation. |
| **UI labels A–J change for localization** | Keep internal `Coordinate(rowIndex,columnIndex)` numeric; derive display labels from `GridSpec`. |
| **Player disconnects during own turn** | No game-rule result should occur merely from transport loss. Reconnection/inactivity expiration is product policy. |
| **Both browser clients fire concurrently** | Aggregate version/transaction locking must allow only the command valid for the active turn. The losing concurrent command receives a stale-state/not-your-turn error. |
| **Client submits fabricated “hit” result** | Never accept it. Client sends target coordinate; authoritative server calculates result from hidden fleet state. |
| **Refresh/reconnect** | Reconstruct the exact private projection from the server using the seat credential, not from trusted browser state. |
| **Opponent sinks a ship adjacent to another in Hasbro mode** | Do not reveal or mark surrounding cells automatically; that would leak potentially occupied coordinates. |
| **Salvo contains duplicate coordinates** | Recommendation: reject the salvo as invalid before resolving any attack, avoiding partial side effects. |
| **Salvo sinks the final ship before later declared shots** | Must be defined per Salvo version. Modern sequential Hasbro wording and historical 1931 delayed resolution are not identical, so do not invent one universal behavior. citeturn31view2turn10view0 |
| **Resignation on same network tick as final shot** | Serialize commands against aggregate version; exactly one terminal cause becomes authoritative. |
| **Game TTL expires while a request is in flight** | Apply a deterministic transaction ordering; once `EXPIRED`, firing cannot reopen the game. |
| **Reveal fleets after game** | Product choice, not required by classic rules. Make it an explicit privacy/display policy rather than assuming all hidden state becomes public. |

### Verified conflicts and unresolved questions

**Original 1931 Salvo versus modern “Salvo.”** These are definitely not one mechanic. The primary Starex scan has four ships, six initial shots, diagonal placement and weighted firepower loss; modern Hasbro Salvo begins with one shot per ship afloat. Any historical summary that collapses these into “the Salvo rule” loses material information. The primary documents should control the implementation. citeturn10view0turn10view1turn31view2

**Exact original 1967 text.** The Strong establishes that Milton Bradley published plastic *Battleship* in 1967, while Hasbro provides a later “Retro Series 1967 Edition.” That is good evidence for the lineage, but the present research did not obtain an independently scanned original 1967 instruction sheet suitable for a line-by-line comparison. Accordingly, `MILTON_BRADLEY_1967_EXACT` would be premature; `HASBRO_RETRO_1967_B7744` is the defensible source-specific label for those reproduced rules. citeturn30view0turn3view1

**Touching in classic Milton Bradley/Hasbro.** The rulebooks explicitly prohibit overlap and diagonal ship placement but do not state “ships may touch.” Therefore the conclusion that touching is legal is a strong rules interpretation based on the absence of a spacing prohibition, not an express publisher sentence. For software fidelity, the safest approach is to model exactly what is prohibited and **not invent** the one-cell Sea Battle halo. citeturn2view0turn31view2

**Repeated shots.** Hasbro instructs players to mark coordinates so they do not call the same shot again but does not specify what happens if they nevertheless do so. The browser game therefore needs a house/application rule. Rejecting the duplicate without consuming the turn is the cleanest choice because it does not allow a client mistake to manufacture a false second hit. citeturn31view2

**“Ukrainian rules.”** There is no verified official national standard behind that project name. Available Ukrainian first-party commercial evidence actually demonstrates multiple incompatible games under *Морський бій*. The ten-ship/no-touch/hit-continues profile is a well-attested customary rule family, but the evidence does not justify calling it the uniquely Ukrainian edition. citeturn26view0turn26view1turn28view1turn22search1

**The age of Sea Battle.** The 1907 diary establishes use of the phrase/game name before World War I, while museum history describes a similar hand-drawn-grid game among Russian officers during World War I. These sources are compatible if interpreted cautiously: there was pre-WWI/WWI Russian-language Sea Battle play, but neither source proves that its fleet counts, adjacency rule or hit-retains-turn behavior matched today's customary version. citeturn13search1turn30view0

**Current Hasbro “Classic” is not a sufficient immutable ID.** Hasbro itself has marketed a four-ship B1817 product as the classic naval-combat game while other/current classic products use five ships. Therefore a persisted `edition = HASBRO_CLASSIC` is not enough to reconstruct game state years later. Use a stable ruleset version/product profile. citeturn31view2turn23view0

**Recommended final decision:** replace `UKRAINIAN` and bare `MILTON_BRADLEY` with versioned behavioral rulesets. For the application's initial release, the cleanest canonical pair is **`SEA_BATTLE_10_SHIP`** and **`HASBRO_CLASSIC_2002`**, adding **`MILTON_BRADLEY_1990`** only when exact historical fleet naming matters. Keep Salvo as its own versioned ruleset rather than a universal feature flag. This structure follows the documentary evidence and, more importantly for the packaged browser/JAR architecture, prevents future editions from forcing incompatible rules into hard-coded `if edition == ...` behavior.
